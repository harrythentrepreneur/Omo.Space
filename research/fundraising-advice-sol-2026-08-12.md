## Bottom line

Your strategic conclusion is right, but the financing ranges mix two different realities:

- The probability of raising is lower than your ranges imply. For Omo alone, the most likely institutional outcome is **$0**.
- If a round does come together, your **$3M–$5M SAFE caps are slightly low** for the 2026 market.
- PhonicsMaker improves Omo’s credibility and ability to close a round more than it mathematically increases valuation.
- **Keep PhonicsMaker and use its actual free cash flow to prove Omo. Do not sell it for $40k without much stronger evidence that it is barely profitable or collapsing.**

The 2026 market is barbelled: AI receives enormous attention, but capital is concentrating into fewer winners. AI captured 50% of pre-seed dollars in Q1 2026, while seed funding in H1 2026 fell from $6.5B to $3.8B year over year. Omo has the right label but not yet the evidence needed to benefit from the premium. ## 2. The two scenarios

### A. Omo alone

Current evidence: single-digit catalog, no production Stripe, no paid users, no marketplace liquidity and no repeat cohort.

**Realistically closeable:** $50k–$150k from founder-network angels, friends/family or a small accelerator. A compelling founder with unusually strong investor access might stretch to $250k–$300k. Most pre-seed funds will pass.

**Terms I would use:**

- Standard **post-money SAFE**
- **$5M cap**, no discount
- Target **$100k**
- Hard maximum **$150k**
- Dilution at the cap: **2% at $100k; 3% at $150k**
- Pro-rata rights only for a meaningful lead investor, not every small angel

Do not cut the cap to $3M merely to rescue a weak process. Investors who do not believe in the business generally will not change their minds over a $2M cap reduction; you will mainly give away more ownership.

### B. Omo with PhonicsMaker retained as the wedge

PhonicsMaker improves the story only if Omo has legally reliable access to the product, brand, users and economics—and the figures survive diligence.

Before Omo conversion evidence, I would underwrite:

- **$200k–$400k**
- Target **$300k**
- Standard post-money SAFE
- **$7.5M cap**, no discount
- Dilution: **4% at $300k; 5.3% at $400k**
- Hard maximum **$500k**, producing 6.7% dilution

A $750k close at an $8M cap sells 9.4% before the next round. That is not disastrous, but it is unnecessary dilution when PhonicsMaker can finance the pilot.

The wedge does not make Omo a $60k-ARR company. It provides:

- Evidence that the founder has built and monetized something
- A warm, relevant launch channel
- Reduced customer-acquisition risk
- Runway and negotiating leverage
- A credible first category

If Omo does not legally own or have binding rights to that channel, investors may value it at effectively zero.

## 3. What to do with PhonicsMaker

The ranking is:

1. **Use actual free cash flow to bootstrap Omo**
2. **Use it as supporting evidence and distribution proof**
3. **Sell only if the underlying economics are genuinely poor**

The proposed $40k value deserves much more scrutiny. Acquire.com’s January 2026 report says profitable SaaS acquisitions sold at a median **3.9× annual profit**, not 0.7× ARR. [Acquire.com 2026 multiples report](https://blog.acquire.com/acquire-com-biannual-acquisition-multiples-report-jan-2026/).

At that benchmark:

- $40k corresponds to only about **$10.3k annual profit**, or **$855/month**
- $2k monthly normalized profit suggests roughly **$94k**
- $3k monthly normalized profit suggests roughly **$140k**

PhonicsMaker may deserve a large haircut for decline, platform dependence, weak retention, support burden or obsolete code. But “AI wrapper” is not enough to infer $40k.

Sell at approximately $40k only if:

- Normalized profit after replacement support is below roughly $1k/month;
- Revenue is declining rapidly;
- The list is stale or legally unusable for the Omo pilot; or
- Operating it prevents the founder from executing and no affordable operator can take it over.

Otherwise, $40k buys only eight months of current gross revenue and destroys Omo’s best distribution asset.

Use **free cash flow, not MRR**, for planning. First deduct COGS, refunds, support, taxes and a PhonicsMaker operating reserve. A sensible Omo pilot budget is **$5k–$10k over 90 days**, not the entire $5k monthly top line.

## 4. The right instrument and what to avoid

Use a standard post-money valuation-cap SAFE. Current Carta data shows SAFEs remain the default at pre-seed; convertible notes represented only 7% of Q1 2026 pre-seed rounds. [Carta Q1 2026](https://carta.com/data/state-of-pre-seed-q1-2026/). Post-money SAFEs also make the sold ownership easy to calculate: investment divided by cap. [YC SAFE documents](https://www.ycombinator.com/documents).

Avoid:

- A priced round below roughly $750k–$1M unless a credible lead insists
- Convertible debt with maturity, interest or personal guarantees
- Both a low cap and a 20% discount
- Uncapped SAFEs
- Multiple rolling SAFE caps that become impossible to model
- Board seats, veto rights or full financial-control rights for a small angel check
- Full-ratchet anti-dilution, participating preferences or redemption rights
- Revenue-share financing that sweeps PhonicsMaker’s scarce cash flow
- Security over the IP or founder guarantees
- Calling GMV, customer spend or PhonicsMaker revenue “Omo revenue”

## 5. Important issues you are missing

**The narrative:** “SaaS is dying” hurts the raise. It makes the founder sound ideological, contradicts the cash-generating asset he owns and invites investors to ask why Omo itself will not be displaced. Use:

> Subscriptions remain right for continuous systems of record. Omo targets intermittent, outcome-defined AI jobs where per-result pricing and portability are better.

**Solo-founder discount:** Solo founders are increasingly common, but they received disproportionately less capital; Carta reports solo-founded companies represented 30% of 2024 formations but only 14.7% of priced-round capital. [Carta solo-founder report](https://carta.com/uk/en/data/solo-founders-report/). Here the discount is especially relevant because QA, refunds, creator disputes, payouts and support are core marketplace functions. The first incremental hire should probably be part-time operations/customer support, not another engineer.

**Terminology:** Do not call the 4,500 addresses “subscribers” if they are email accounts, former users or leads. Report separately:

- Current paying accounts
- Active users
- Marketable opted-in emails
- Deliverable emails
- Recent opens/clicks
- Churned customers

**Corporate ownership:** Before fundraising, investors must see one clean chain of rights. Omo should own its IP, and either own PhonicsMaker or have a binding, exclusive right to operate Omo Education and reach eligible customers. Keep separate product-level books even if both ultimately sit under one parent.

**Email-list legality:** Moving from PhonicsMaker to a differently branded Omo marketplace is not automatically permitted everywhere. UK rules, for example, restrict the customer “soft opt-in” to the same organization marketing similar products, with opt-outs at collection and in every message. Launch through the PhonicsMaker identity and have privacy counsel review the list. [ICO guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/).

**Tax:** If targeting U.S. venture capital, get startup tax counsel before contributing PhonicsMaker assets to an Omo C-corp. Address IP assignment, asset-transfer tax, liabilities, founder-stock vesting, the 30-day §83(b) deadline and QSBS eligibility. The current IRS gross-asset threshold for qualifying stock issued after July 4, 2025 is $75M, but the exact structure and holding period matter. [IRS guidance](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040sd).

## Clear recommendation

**Keep PhonicsMaker, do not fundraise yet, and spend at most $5k–$10k of its true free cash flow on a 90-day Omo Education pilot.**

Raise only after Omo demonstrates:

- Production payments and automatic failure refunds
- At least 100 paying accounts
- At least 30% 30-day paid repeat
- At least 95% accepted-output success
- Under 5% refunds or serious complaints
- Approximately $1k monthly Omo contribution for two to three consecutive months

At that point, target **$300k–$500k on a $10M post-money SAFE**, selling 3%–5%. If personal runway makes waiting impossible, take only a **$100k–$150k bridge at a $5M–$6M post-money cap**.

So: your recommendation is substantially correct. The largest correction is that PhonicsMaker may be worth materially more than $40k, while Omo’s current ability to attract institutional capital is materially weaker than the headline AI market suggests.
