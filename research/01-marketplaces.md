# Online marketplaces for AI prompts, skills, agents and automations

**Research date:** 2026-08-08  
**Scope:** Public online catalogs and stores found through broad web discovery, then checked against first-party pages where reachable. This is **as complete as found**, not a claim of internet-wide completeness. “Marketplace” is used broadly; many products below are free directories, template galleries, deployment catalogs, or enterprise procurement channels rather than two-sided cash marketplaces.

## Evidence and notation

- **OFFICIAL** — first-party product, documentation, terms, pricing, catalog, or repository page.
- **NEWS** — dated reporting/announcement.
- **DIRECTORY** — third-party directory/catalog page.
- **REVIEW** — third-party analysis/review.
- **UNVERIFIED** — requested fact was not exposed on a reachable official page; no number is inferred.
- Prices are public list prices observed or explicitly stated in cited pages; enterprise/private prices are not guessed. “Free” means free to browse/install, not necessarily free model/API/hosting usage.

### Detail notes

- **PromptBase is the clearest direct competitor.** Its first-party sell page explicitly says “Sell a prompt or skill,” accepts `SKILL.md`, and publishes the unusually creator-friendly **0% direct-link / 20% marketplace** split. Zoneless pays USDC daily with no minimum; scheduled Stripe payouts require $30 and can be weekly/monthly depending on account eligibility. [OFFICIAL](https://promptbase.com/sell) [OFFICIAL](https://promptbase.com/knowledge-base/payouts)
- **PromptsIdeas** is a genuine priced catalog, but the terms page was intermittently unreachable, so its commission and payout timing remain UNVERIFIED. The market page itself exposed the named $4.99/$3.99/free examples. [OFFICIAL](https://promptsideas.com/market)
- **PromptHero, AIPRM and current FlowGPT should not be modeled as classic unit-sale markets.** They monetize audience access, generation, membership, or the surrounding SaaS; creator cash payouts are absent or not transparent on reachable official pages.
- **ChatX and PromptDen are cautionary precedents:** ChatX visibly pivoted away from prompts, while PromptDen could not be reliably reached.

### Detail notes

- **GPT Store has distribution but not a predictable seller business model.** The original official announcement promised earnings based on engagement; the current help route does not expose a universal rate card, commission, or per-install price. Treat revenue as invite/experiment dependent, not bankable GMV.
- **Poe** is the most concrete assistant creator economy: prompt and server bots may dynamically set per-message prices, plus receive subscription-conversion earnings. It is usage monetization, not transfer of a portable bot artifact.
- **Gemini Gems** supports sharing and editing through Drive-style permissions but has no public store, paid listing, or creator payout. It is a feature gap/opportunity, not a current marketplace competitor.
- **Microsoft and Salesforce** are procurement stores: attractive for enterprise reach, but publishers sell packaged SaaS/agents under platform commercial agreements rather than $5 prompt files.

# Cross-market conclusions and gaps

1. **True creator marketplaces are rare.** PromptBase is the clearest artifact-sale market with a published 0%/20% fee structure; Poe is the clearest usage-revenue assistant platform. Most others monetize hosting, subscription seats, runs, tasks or inference—not the artifact creator.
2. **Hosted execution and creator commerce remain separated.** n8n, Dify, Relevance AI, Gumloop, Vercel, Replicate and AWS provide runtime/deployment, but usually distribute templates free. PromptBase sells artifacts but does not bundle production-grade hosted operations.
3. **Portable cross-framework skills are the largest open gap.** Anthropic’s `SKILL.md` format and aggregators create abundant supply, but portability, compatibility testing, dependency pinning, security review, updates, support/SLA and paid hosted execution are not packaged together.
4. **MCP registries are discovery commodities.** Counts are duplicated and quality varies; differentiation is moving toward scanning, verified publishers, managed OAuth/secrets, remote hosting, usage analytics and per-call payments.
5. **Creator economics are opaque.** GPT Store, enterprise agent stores, CrewAI and most workflow galleries publish no stable take rate. A new entrant can differentiate through explicit revenue share, escrow/refunds, transparent metering and predictable payout cadence.
6. **Examples/pricing are often not durable.** Many catalogs are client-rendered, account-gated or usage-priced. This report marks those values UNVERIFIED rather than fabricating static listing prices.
7. **Enterprise stores prove willingness to transact, but with heavy packaging.** Microsoft, Salesforce and AWS support procurement, security review and contracts; they are channels for vendors, not low-friction creator marketplaces.

# Method and limitations

- Discovery used broad platform/category queries, then direct HTTP retrieval of official catalog, documentation, pricing and terms pages on 2026-08-08.
- URLs were normalized and deduplicated in the accompanying source ledger. Cross-listed platforms (PromptBase skills, Salesforce AgentExchange) are intentionally referenced in multiple analytical categories but have one canonical source URL per ledger row.
- Cloudflare/region/JavaScript blocks limited direct inspection of OpenAI pages, Agent.ai, Agentverse, Make, PulseMCP, Glama and some terms pages. The report labels these limitations and avoids inventing counts or percentages.
- Listing totals are self-reported snapshots and change rapidly; overlapping registries should never be summed.
- No claim of internet-wide completeness is made: this is **as complete as found** as of the stated research date.
