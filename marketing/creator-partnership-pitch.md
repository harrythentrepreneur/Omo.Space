# Creator partnership pitch (refined)

Lead with audience value + the open-source differentiator + the money. Two
mechanics, stated separately. This replaces the economics line in
creator-dm-copy.md.

## Master DM template

> Hi [name] — I'm building Omo, an open-source marketplace of proven teacher
> workflows. Every workflow is free to download and self-host — you bring your
> own AI keys. For teachers who don't want that setup, we host it and charge a
> few cents to a few dollars per result, no subscription, no lock-in.
>
> I'd like to partner: every teacher who signs up through you gets $5 of credit
> to try it, and you keep 95% of anything they spend. If there's a better way
> to work together, I'm open to ideas.
>
> — Harry, founder of Omo

## Mission line (one sentence, for the DM opener or About)

> Most AI tools charge you every month whether you use them or not. We're
> open-sourcing the workflows and charging a few cents only when you need the
> result — so any teacher can use the good stuff without a subscription.

Rules it honors: "free" is banned (compute costs money — the access is free, the
result is a few cents). "Remove subscriptions" is banned (overreach — same trap
as "SaaS is dying"). The winnable claim is the subscription TAX: paying monthly
for a tool you use twice. Availability-to-everybody is the true part and it
survives via open-source + no lock-in + cents-per-result.

## The full menu (state separately, only if they reply)

1. Affiliate (refer your audience): you keep 95% of referred sales, and every
   referred signup gets $5 of credit. Cap the credit per creator (first 50
   signups, time-boxed 30 days) until unit economics are proven.
2. List your own workflow (creator): you keep 85% of every hosted run of your
   workflow, after direct run costs. This is where the open-source angle pays
   off — open-source the skill.md, we host it, you earn on every run.

## Rules this honors (locked)

- Never say the output is "free" — the code is free, the compute is not
  ("you bring your own AI keys").
- "a few cents to a few dollars," never "free" / "cheap."
- "open-source marketplace," not "community platform" — we have no community
  features yet; don't overclaim.
- Keep a tight menu; never say "any type of collaboration" (cash is ~$200 —
  we can't buy sponsored posts).
- Credits are real COGS: cap + time-box per creator.

## Open decisions (depend on Harry)

- Credit framing: "$5 = 24 books" (locked in GTM) vs the current $0.10 tools
  ($5 = ~50 word lists) vs books at $0.30-1 ($5 = 5-16 books). Fix the unit
  before the number goes in front of a creator.
- Sequence: still gated behind GATE 1. Do not send until a teacher pays twice
  and checkout is proven.
