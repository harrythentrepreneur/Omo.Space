## 1. Precise style synthesis

### Shared visual formula

These covers behave like bright retail packaging for a classroom resource. A huge headline identifies the product at a glance; a dense fan or flat lay of printable pages proves what is included; saturated color blocks create shelf impact; and heavy black outlines keep everything legible at thumbnail size. The result is practical, abundant, cheerful, and teacher-made rather than sleek, corporate, or software-like.

### Color palette

Use a white-dominant field with near-black structure and a small set of hard, saturated accents. Approximate working colors sampled or visually matched from the references are:

- Paper white: `#FFFFFF`
- Warm off-white: `#FAFAF7`
- Near-black type and outlines: `#111111`
- Orange-red headline: `#F4511E`
- Hot pink/magenta: `#F7088A`
- Bright cyan: `#08C9E8`
- School-bus yellow: `#FFD21A`
- Tangerine: `#FF7A24`
- Lime/chartreuse: `#8BCB00`
- Warm kraft tan: `#B89263`
- Soft smoke gray: `#A6A19F`
- Pale pink grounding accent: `#F7A6C5`

Keep approximately 65–75% of the image white or grayscale. Use three to five accent colors as solid fills in title letters, bands, worksheet backing mats, and small props. Do not use gradients, neon glow, metallic colors, beige lifestyle grading, or muted pastel-only palettes.

### Typography and title treatment

- Treat the title as the primary illustration, not as a small label.
- Use an extra-bold, condensed classroom/display sans-serif: tall Impact-, Anton-, or hand-cut teacher-display energy, without copying a branded font exactly.
- Set the title in uppercase with tight tracking and compact line spacing.
- Make the title occupy roughly the upper 28–34% of the portrait card and remain unmistakable at a 180-pixel-wide preview.
- Use high-contrast solid fills with a thick `#111111` outline. The outline should feel about 4–7% of cap height, with a small soft gray or black offset shadow for lift.
- Two verified title systems are compatible: one enormous orange-red outlined headline on white, or stacked headline bands combining white-on-magenta, individually colored outlined letters, and black-on-cyan type.
- Avoid thin type, sentence case, elegant editorial fonts, script, gradients inside letters, bevels, chrome effects, and SaaS-style pill labels.

### Layout and composition

- Build an edge-to-edge cover with very little unused outer margin.
- Reserve the top 28–34% for a three-line title stack. The title must be cleanly separated from the product previews and never sit on a visually noisy photo.
- Fill the lower 66–72% with three to five overlapping portrait worksheet sheets. Make one front sheet the large hero and rotate the supporting pages only about 2–7 degrees.
- Crop peripheral sheets and props confidently at the frame edges so the product feels abundant, not delicately centered.
- Use thin cyan, magenta, yellow, lime, or orange backing mats behind selected sheets to make the layers read immediately.
- Put one expressive phonics-specific mascot or hero icon in a lower corner, overlapping the page bundle without obscuring it.
- A thin rounded near-black outer keyline is optional. Strong rectangular header/footer bands are welcome; glossy badges and app-interface chrome are not.
- Compose specifically for `360 × 640` pixels (`9:16`). Do not design for a square crop or leave a large empty UI-copy zone.

### Illustration, photo, and surface treatment

- The printable pages should use black-and-white elementary worksheet art: rounded shapes, friendly faces, bold contours, large markable areas, and simple visual activities.
- Flat vector/cartoon rendering is the dominant language. Real school supplies may be lightly photorealistic, but they must use bright diffuse overhead light and simple matte surfaces.
- Use approximately 3–5-pixel-equivalent black lines within the worksheet previews and 6–10-pixel-equivalent lines on the foreground mascot at the 360 × 640 target.
- Add only narrow contact shadows or soft gray drop shadows beneath overlapping pages and supplies. Avoid dramatic directional lighting, glossy 3D rendering, painterly texture, grain, depth-of-field blur, and environmental classroom scenes.
- For generation reliability, allow readable typography only in the main title. Worksheet previews should communicate through pictures, blank answer shapes, circles, matching paths, and line art—not generated microcopy.

### Borders, bands, and badges

- Use flat rectangular bands as structural packaging devices: hot pink behind white title text, cyan beneath black title text, or a black footer with white text when a secondary label is genuinely needed.
- Use thick black outlines around display letters and simple black keylines around worksheet pages.
- Keep borders crisp and flat. Avoid glassmorphism, rounded UI cards, floating chips, ribbon seals, gold stamps, and glossy promotional bursts.
- For this thumbnail, omit extra badges and footer copy so the exact product title is the only readable text.

### Background and mood

The background should be white, bright, matte, and almost texture-free. The mood is energetic, friendly, organized, low-prep, and immediately useful for kindergarten through early elementary teachers. It should resemble proven classroom-resource packaging, not an AI tool, app dashboard, luxury editorial cover, or cinematic scene.

### What is fixed versus flexible

Fixed elements are the white-dominant palette, huge outlined uppercase title, saturated classroom accents, dense worksheet bundle, heavy near-black linework, flat color, simple phonics imagery, and small-thumbnail readability. Flexible elements are whether the props are vector or lightly photographic, whether the mascot is large or small, whether the title uses one color or alternating colors, and whether a thin outer border is present.
