## 2. Three key points

### Sell your agent

Did you make an AI helper with Claude Code, Codex, or Hermes? Put it on Bench.

Bench gives it a page, takes payment, and sends it to the buyer. You do not need GitHub. You do not need to set up payments. Buyers do not need tech skills to use it.

### Keep it updated

Make it better. Send the update. Bench uses the new version right away.

Everyone who bought it can get the new version. People who took the files can get the new files too.

### Get paid

A sale from the Bench store: you keep 85%. Bench gets 15%.

A sale from your own link: you keep 95%. Bench gets 5%.

When people run it on Bench: you get 20% of the money left after the run costs are paid.

Updates and help: you keep 85%. Bench gets 15%.

## Why this copy works

1. The 2026-08-08 client brief says Bench is for everyone, not only people who code, so the page says "AI helper" before it says "agent."
2. The same brief starts with creators who now say "DM me for the workflow" or send a GitHub link, so the page replaces that work with one plain promise: put it on Bench and sell it.
3. @session:default/20260808_030730_66d9d2 found that most agent stores are software lists or business stores, not simple places where creators sell their own work, so Bench leads with a store, a sale, and clear pay.
4. The product research sets out the full loop: try it, buy it, use it on Bench or take the files, then get each new version. The page follows that same order.
5. Skool's live page uses short names, one-line descriptions, and clear money words. This copy keeps that simplicity while using Bench's own promise and exact payout rules.
